# Practical caveats

## What public data can and cannot do

- SEC disclosures are authoritative filing records, not audited investment conclusions or a guarantee that every fact will be easy to compare. They can be amended, restated, tagged differently, or reported under different accounting and fiscal calendars.
- XBRL is structured disclosure data, not a fully normalized analyst database. SEC companyfacts covers selected non-custom-taxonomy facts that apply to the whole filing entity. Custom tags, segment details, unusual contexts, and some issuer-specific definitions may require manual filing review. Calendar-aligned frames may not match fiscal quarters.
- A company's IR release or slide deck is primary evidence of management's presentation, but adjusted measures, forecasts, and event dates should be labelled as company statements and reconciled where possible. Forward-looking statements are uncertain.
- Free quote pages can be delayed or have unclear timestamps. A quote should never be called live/current unless the source establishes that. Market capitalization also depends on the share-count date and security class.
- Public peer data may have inconsistent definitions and stale prices. Recalculate key multiples from filed numbers when feasible; disclose approximations.
- Web search coverage and page access vary by host and region. If a source is unavailable, state the limitation and ask for the document or use an explicitly dated fallback.

## Financial-model judgement

- Normalized earnings and maintenance capex are estimates. Acquisitions, commodity cycles, seasonality, pricing, and working-capital swings can make a short history misleading.
- Stock-based compensation is a real economic cost even when non-cash in the period. Consider dilution or an explicit expense assumption.
- Debt-like claims may include leases, pensions, preferred stock, guarantees, or minority interests. Enterprise-to-equity bridges must be consistent with the cash flows and multiples used.
- A DCF can be highly sensitive to discount rates and terminal assumptions. If most of value comes from terminal value, say so. Scenario ranges describe assumptions, not probabilities unless there is a sourced basis.
- Multiples can embed different growth, risk, capital intensity, accounting, or rates. A peer median is not automatically fair value.
- Share splits, ADR ratios, converts, options, buybacks, issuance, and recent events may cause per-share values to be stale or inconsistent.

## Safe interpretation

The result is general educational research. It does not account for personal objectives, time horizon, tax status, liquidity needs, concentration, or risk capacity. Do not translate it into a personalized trade instruction. Users should independently verify the sources and consult qualified professionals where appropriate.
